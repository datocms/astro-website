# Pixel videos

`<pixel-video>` plays `.pxv` files (`src/lib/pixel-video/format.ts`), not `.mp4` files. The files are in `public/videos/` and shown by `PageCloser` (the footer). Each file gives all its look (see "The file gives the look" below): a page gives only its URL, `<PageCloser video="/videos/hand-ok.pxv" />`. The scripts of `scripts/pixel-videos/` make a `.pxv`: from green screen footage (below) or from a Lottie animation (see "Lottie animations"). `/dev/pxv-gallery` shows all the files. The commands are in `scripts/pixel-videos/README.md`.

## Footage on a flat background (the pattern for hands and people)

Source: green-screen footage of Green Screen Animation on YouTube. The files have green, blue or black backgrounds, and title cards between the takes.

The `.pxv` file is the only copy that we keep. Do not keep a recipe (a list of clips with their settings, a script for one video) to make the files again: change a file with `meta.ts` or `resize.ts`, or make a new one. Each file records `source`, the URL of its footage (a YouTube video at the start of the take), only to find the footage again. The commands are in `scripts/pixel-videos/README.md`.

1. **Find the take**: `key.py runs <url>` downloads the video (best resolution and bitrate: some are 4K, the 1080p streams are often below 1 Mbit/s) to `tmp/source/`, and lists its runs. A run is a take with a subject on a flat background. Some videos have number cards on black between the takes, and they look like a run: add `--only green`. The same takes repeat on each background: choose different takes, and prefer green or blue to black (a luma key also removes the dark shadows of the subject).
2. **Key it**: `key.py <url> --run <n> --name <name>`. It writes:
   - `tmp/<name>.mov`: the take with a transparent background (ProRes 4444; QuickTime and QuickLook show the alpha). Whole run, every frame, the frame rate of the source, no loop, cropped to the union of the boxes of the subject over all the frames.
   - `tmp/<name>.png`: 8 frames of the .mov on a checkerboard. Look at it first: a bad key shows here. Correct with `--lo`, `--hi`, `--despill`, and the frames with `--trim <first> <last>`.
     It prints the URL of the take, for step 3.
3. **Encode**: `encode.ts tmp/<name>.mov public/videos/<name>.pxv --height 80 --tones otsu --source <url> --preview tmp/<name>.pxv.png --palettes tmp/<name>.palettes.png`. Height 80 for hands, 120 for full bodies. It writes the file, the cells (`.pxv.png`: dark grey, grey, white, checkerboard for empty) and the same frames in the colours of the footer (`.palettes.png`, purple theme: top row `tonal`, bottom row `onAccent`). It prints the edges and `dark`, the share of the subject in the background layer. Change the thresholds with `--t-mid` and `--t-high`, the edges with `--edges`.
4. **Choose the palette**: with `dark` of 30% or more, look at the `.palettes.png` with the user, ask which row they prefer, and set it with `meta.ts <file> --set palette=onAccent` (or `--palette` of `encode.ts`). The choice is by eye: do not set it without the user.
5. **Look** at it in `/dev/pxv-gallery`: it shows all the files of `public/videos/`, each one in a footer. Then give the page the file: `<PageCloser video="/videos/<name>.pxv" />`.

The rules of the process, and do not change them without a reason:

- **The key is a colour difference key**, in numpy at full resolution: for green, `G - max(R, B)` divided by the same value of the screen. Skin, grey, black and white have no green excess, thus they stay opaque. Do not use ffmpeg `chromakey`: it compares 4:2:0 chroma with a distance, it removes grey and pale clothes (a whole grey-shirted dancer became transparent), and it gives blocky edges.
- **Do not change the picture**: no blur, no grey, no tone curve, no contrast. Only the despill (green or blue above `max` of the two other channels is removed). A tone curve made the hands look plastic, and the pxv has only 3 layers, thus a curve does the same as other thresholds. Change the thresholds, not the pixels.
- **Thresholds**: `--tones otsu` splits the opaque pixels into the three most distinct groups of tones (Otsu with three classes). A dancer gets dark trousers, a light shirt and a face with features. A hand gets three bands of shading. `--tones thirds` gives each layer a third of the pixels. Do not use the median of all the pixels (`--skin`): dark clothes pull it down, and then the face and the shirt are one flat white.
- **Size**: use `--height`, not `--max-side`. The footer draws each video at the same height (494 px), thus the same height in cells gives the same size of square on screen. Each cell is one square.
- **Palette**: the background of a clip stays transparent, and the band of the footer shows the ground of the palette, also out of the video (`extend` of the palette, true for all but `silhouette`). With `tonal` (the default) the ground is dark and is the fill of the background layer, thus the dark part of the subject (black clothes) does not show. `onAccent` (`src/lib/pixel-video/styles.ts`) has its own ground (`groundFill`, the accent colour) and draws the background layer as dark squares on it. Do not make the background opaque to fix a dark subject (a box in the band): change the palette. Many dark cells do not always mean `onAccent`: a dark suit can read well as the shape that the light parts make.
- **Edges**: a `.pxv` file (format `PXV3`) records the edges of the video that cut the subject (`Edge` in `format.ts`): an arm that comes in from the right has `right`. With `extend`, the band shows the ground out of the video, thus a cut edge in the middle of the band shows as a straight line. `<pixel-video>` puts the edges in `data-edges`, and `PageCloser` keeps a cut side edge on the edge of the band. With `left` and `right`, it scales the video up to the width of the band and the band cuts the top (larger squares). `top` and `bottom` are always on the band. `encode()` finds the edges (`findEdges()`: the subject touches 0.5% or more of the cells of the edge) when they are not given. `encode.ts` prints the part of each edge that the subject touches. An opaque video gets no edges: set them by hand with `scripts/pixel-videos/meta.ts <file> --set edges=right`.
- **The file gives the look**: the settings of a `.pxv` (JSON in the header) are only two: `palette` (a name of `palettes` in `styles.ts`, never colours) and `focus` (the point of the subject, 0–1 from the left, that stays in the centre when the band cuts the sides). The palette gives the colours, the surface and its switches (`flatBackground`, `extend`). All the videos have the same rest of the look: one square for each cell and the same lens under the pointer, measured in cells (`LENS` in `renderer.ts`). The lens is a switch of the element, not of the file or of the palette: `<PixelVideo lens={false}>` (attribute `no-lens`) removes it, for a video that is not a thing to touch. `hold` is also a switch of the element: `<PixelVideo hold={[38, 47]}>` (attribute `hold="38-47"`) plays the video once up to the last frame, then goes back and forth in that range at a quarter of the speed, for a gesture that stays in its pose. Under reduced motion it shows the last frame of the range. Give a range with small steps, and frames that show the pose. In development, `PixelVideo` shows a tag (`DevConfig`) at the top right corner when the pointer is on the video (`PageCloser` puts it on the band, because the band can cut the video): the pointer on the tag shows the file, the palette, the focus, the lens, the cells, the frames, the edges and the source. Do not add a setting for one video: when a video needs a different look, add a palette. The `palette` attribute of the element replaces the palette of the file (for a video that a component shows on another surface). The palette also gives the surface of the footer (`surface` of the palette): the copy and the buttons above the video use it. The server reads the header of each file with no fetch: the module `virtual:pxv-headers` (plugin `pxvHeaders` in `astro.config.ts`, helpers in `headers.ts`). Set them in the file with `scripts/pixel-videos/meta.ts <file> --set key=value`, which also shows them. A change of the colours of a palette in `styles.ts` changes all the files of that palette.
- **Source**: `source` in the JSON of the header (not a setting) is the URL of the footage: for YouTube, the video at the start of the take (`&t=`). `encode.ts --source`, `meta.ts --set source=<url>`. For a Lottie animation, give the URL of the JSON. A file with no `source` has footage that we cannot find again.
- **Format**: the reader reads only `PXV3`. All the files of `public/videos/` are `PXV3`. Do not add a new format version without a conversion of all the files.
- **Resolution**: fewer cells give larger pixels. When the .mov is in `tmp/`, encode it again with another `--height`. Else use `scripts/pixel-videos/resize.ts <in> <out> --height <n>` (or `--scale <0-1>`): `downscale()` in `format.ts` averages the layers of each box of cells (a cell is empty when half of its box or more is empty). The settings do not change.
- A transparent pixel becomes an empty cell: nothing shows there. `--matte '#rrggbb'` puts it on a colour in place.

Keep `tmp/` (Git ignores it): the downloaded sources, the .mov files and the sheets. They are a cache, not a recipe.

## Lottie animations

`scripts/pixel-videos/lottie.py` renders a Lottie JSON (a file, a URL, or `noto:<codepoint>` for an animated Noto emoji) with rlottie into `tmp/<name>.mov` with a transparent background, cropped to the box of the drawing in all the frames plus a margin (`--pad`, 3%). Then do steps 3 to 5 above, with the URL of the JSON as `--source`. The margin keeps the drawing off the edges of the video: without it, `findEdges()` finds edges that cut the subject.

## Pitfalls

- A browser has approximately 16 WebGL contexts, and each `<pixel-video>` uses one. A page with many videos must mount them only when near the screen (see `<lazy-footer>` in `src/dev/pxv-gallery.astro`). The warning is "Too many active WebGL contexts".
- A ProRes 4444 .mov is large (approximately 30-100 MB for a hand, 550 MB for the 23 s crowd). `key.py` limits its height to 1080 px.
- Dark subjects (a crowd in the dark) have few highlights. For them, look at the `silhouette` palette (`crazy-dance-1.pxv`, `crazy-dance-2.pxv`): a dark subject on a light background. The palette has `extend: false`: the grid does not fill the band.
- Get a licence for the footage before you use it in production. The footage of Green Screen Animation has its own terms.
